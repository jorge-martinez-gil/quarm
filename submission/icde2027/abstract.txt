Data-quality systems count violations, detect errors, and repair cells, but these outputs do not answer a consequential question: how much does each co-occurring defect mechanism matter to the analytical workload that consumes the data? We introduce QuaRM, a reference-based measurement system that represents data quality as a counterfactual risk surface over coalitions of defect mechanisms. A single paired execution surface supports three deliberately distinct products: Shapley accounting that exactly allocates aggregate quality debt; interaction indices that expose non-additivity; and full-context marginal gains that select an intervention for the current joint state. QuaRM evaluates modest mechanism sets exactly and provides a cached permutation estimator with finite-sample control for larger sets.

We evaluate six relational defects against eight analytical SQL queries on three controlled 100K-row star schemas and a 100K-trip snapshot of official NYC taxi data, using 12 paired repetitions for all 64 coalitions. At equal 10% prevalence, aggregate workload debt ranges from 7.19 to 19.20 risk points, pairwise interactions reach 5.90 points, and query-level priorities reverse across joins, temporal rollups, counts, and averages. On NYC, dimension drift accounts for 9.56 points (95% CI 8.45–10.73). Critically, the largest Shapley account is not the best current repair in all three synthetic populations and repairing it increases loss in two. In a controlled 12-mechanism study, 64 permutations recover the leading account in 95.8% of 400 trials while evaluating 12.8% of 4,096 coalitions. Eight ML settings confirm that the estimand is not SQL-specific. QuaRM turns “data quality” from an unqualified score into an auditable claim about a reference, workload, mechanism portfolio, and decision.
